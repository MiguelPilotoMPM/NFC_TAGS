The traps to know

Four everyday tricks — and what to notice.

Quishing · QR + PHISHING

A scammer sticks a fake QR code on a parking meter. You scan it and land on a fake payment page that steals your money and data.

Card skimming · ATMs & TERMINALS

A thin skimmer on the card slot copies your card, while a hidden camera films your PIN.

Contactless theft · NFC

A portable NFC reader — even a phone — can read a contactless card from very close range.

Fake payment pages · PRESSURE

The page looks real and pushes you: a "25% discount", a countdown, a selfie "to verify". Too many requests? Close it.

How they get so much info

One scan can reveal more than you think.

Metadata

Every photo hides data: the phone model, the date, and often the exact GPS location where you took it.

Cookies & the browser

A page can read your browser, time, language and set cookies to track your visits — sometimes it even asks for your location.

Stay safe · 5 rules

Five habits that stop most scams.

Check the QR. Look for a sticker placed on top of the real code.

Don't pay through a random QR. Type the official website yourself.

Shield your card. Use an RFID sleeve or keep cards together.

Watch for skimmers. Wiggle the slot and cover the keypad.

Think before you tap. Selfie, extra data, big "discount"? Close the page.

Know the words

Key vocabulary for the class (B1).

scam — a dishonest trick to take your money or data.
phishing — fake messages that steal passwords or card details.
quishing — phishing through a QR code.
QR code — a square code you scan with your phone.
fake — not real; made to trick you.
suspicious — something that looks wrong or not safe.
to verify — to check that something is real.
skimmer — a device that copies your card at a slot.
to tap — to touch a card or phone to pay.
contactless — pay by tapping, without a PIN.
NFC — short-range wireless used for tapping/paying.
RFID sleeve — a cover that blocks card reading.
metadata — hidden data inside a file or photo.
GPS location — the exact place where something happened.
cookies — small files a website saves to track you.
browser — the app you use to open websites.
pop-up — a window that appears to get your click.
dark pattern — a design that tricks you into "yes".
fraud — a crime of cheating people for money.
data breach — when private data is stolen or leaked.
password — a secret word to protect an account.
2FA — a second code to log in safely.
